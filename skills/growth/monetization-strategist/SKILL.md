# Monetization Strategist

## Purpose
Recommend a sustainable monetization approach that captures demonstrated value while protecting user trust and long-term product health.

## When to use
Use to evaluate a new revenue model, pricing and packaging, free-to-paid boundaries, monetization experiments, or the impact of a pricing change. This Skill provides strategic analysis, not legal, tax, or financial advice.

## Inputs
- Product value proposition, target segments, lifecycle stage, and current usage.
- Current revenue model, prices, costs, conversion, retention, and unit economics, if known.
- Competitive pricing evidence and customer willingness-to-pay research, if available.
- Business objective, constraints, geography, billing model, and acceptable user-experience risks.

## Process
1. **Assess value readiness.** Identify the user outcome being monetized and whether the product has evidence of repeatable value. If value is not yet established, say so and suggest validation before aggressive gating.
2. **Clarify material unknowns.** Ask focused questions if customer, business model, or goal changes the recommendation. Otherwise proceed with assumptions clearly labeled.
3. **Segment by needs and value.** Identify who receives which value, usage intensity, purchasing context, and who pays versus who uses the product.
4. **Compare relevant models.** Evaluate subscription, freemium, usage-based, one-time purchase, advertising, transaction fees, or seat/enterprise pricing only where relevant.
5. **Recommend packaging and pricing logic.** Define the value metric, tier boundaries, free-versus-paid rationale, and pricing hypotheses. Do not invent validated prices or willingness-to-pay data.
6. **Model trade-offs.** Consider revenue, acquisition, conversion, retention, cost to serve, trust, fairness, and complexity. State the preferred trade-off and why.
7. **Design experiments.** Propose tests of pricing presentation, packaging, limits, trial, or upgrade timing with a primary metric, guardrails, and decision criteria. Avoid unethical or misleading price presentation.
8. **Define success measurement.** Select relevant measures such as conversion, net revenue retention, paid churn, contribution margin, expansion, support contacts, and value realization.
9. **Identify risks and mitigations.** Include backlash, involuntary churn, arbitrage, cost spikes, price discrimination concerns, and operational complexity when relevant.

## Expected outputs
- Value and segment analysis.
- Comparison of relevant revenue models and a clear recommendation.
- Pricing/packaging hypotheses with assumptions and validation needs.
- Explicit trade-offs and prioritized risks with mitigations.
- Experiment plan and monetization health metrics.

## Quality checklist
- Monetization is connected to a specific user outcome.
- The recommendation fits the product's maturity and buying context.
- Price points are labeled as hypotheses unless supported by evidence.
- Free and paid boundaries have a clear rationale.
- Tests measure revenue quality and user harm, not conversion alone.
- Economics include cost to serve when relevant.

## Guardrails
- Do not fabricate competitor prices, market benchmarks, customer research, or unit economics.
- Do not recommend dark patterns, hidden fees, deceptive urgency, or obstructive cancellation.
- Do not optimize short-term conversion while ignoring retention, trust, or cost to serve.
- Flag jurisdiction-specific legal, tax, billing, and consumer-protection questions for qualified review.
- Distinguish gross revenue from contribution margin and revenue retention where relevant.

## Definition of done
The team has a reasoned monetization recommendation, the assumptions behind it, a way to validate it, and explicit safeguards for user trust and business health.
