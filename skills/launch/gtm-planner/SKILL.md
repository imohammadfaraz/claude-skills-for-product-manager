# GTM Planner

## Purpose
Turn a product or feature release into a coordinated go-to-market plan with clear audience, message, execution, and success measures.

## When to use
Use for a new product, major feature, market expansion, relaunch, or staged rollout. Scale the plan to the launch's risk, audience, and team capacity.

## Inputs
- Product/feature description, user problem, value proposition, maturity, and release scope.
- Target users, buying roles, use cases, and evidence of demand.
- Launch goal, date or window, geography, pricing, channels, budget, and team owners if known.
- Product readiness, dependencies, support capacity, legal/security constraints, and known risks.

## Process
1. **Confirm launch type and objective.** Identify the decision or outcome the launch should drive. Ask only for material missing information; otherwise state assumptions.
2. **Define target audience.** Prioritize segments by need, fit, access, and evidence. Distinguish users, buyers, influencers, and internal stakeholders where relevant.
3. **Position the offer.** State target audience, problem, differentiated benefit, reasons to believe, and alternatives. Do not claim differentiation without evidence.
4. **Build the message.** Create a core message, proof points, segment-specific framing, objections and responses, and a clear call to action.
5. **Select channels.** Choose channels based on audience reach, credibility, conversion path, cost, and operational capacity. Do not recommend every channel by default.
6. **Sequence launch work.** Plan pre-launch validation, readiness gates, launch-day actions, rollout stages, and post-launch follow-up. Include owner and timing only when supplied or clearly proposed.
7. **Plan activation and retention.** Describe the first value moment, onboarding/support needed, and follow-up that helps users realize value.
8. **Set measurement.** Define leading indicators, outcome metrics, guardrails, data sources, and review cadence. Mark target values as proposed if not evidence-backed.
9. **Map risks and contingencies.** Cover readiness, demand, support load, reliability, privacy/security, messaging, channel, and dependency risks as relevant.
10. **Recommend a launch decision.** State launch prerequisites, unresolved blockers, and what would trigger delay, staged rollout, rollback, or iteration.

## Expected outputs
- Launch objective and audience prioritization.
- Positioning and messaging brief.
- Channel plan with rationale.
- Phased launch checklist with workstreams, owners, dependencies, and dates when known.
- Activation plan, metric framework, readiness gates, risks, and contingency actions.
- Open questions and go/no-go criteria.

## Quality checklist
- Each activity has a purpose and target audience.
- Messaging reflects verified value and avoids unsupported claims.
- Channels are prioritized rather than listed indiscriminately.
- Readiness and rollback criteria are explicit for material risks.
- Metrics connect launch activity to user activation and business outcomes.
- Plan scope fits team capacity and launch risk.

## Guardrails
- Do not invent launch dates, budgets, owners, commitments, or customer proof.
- Do not treat launch activity or impressions as evidence of product success.
- Do not assume public launch is always preferable to a beta or staged rollout.
- Flag legal, privacy, security, accessibility, and operational readiness needs when relevant.
- Distinguish recommendations from confirmed plans.

## Definition of done
The team can align on who the launch serves, what it promises, how it reaches users, what must be ready, and how success or failure will be recognized.
